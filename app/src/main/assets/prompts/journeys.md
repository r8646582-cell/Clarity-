# Journeys — Purpose

Structured journeys, one step per day. The app parses this file: each journey starts with "## ",
followed by a one-line description, then one line per day in the form
"Day N | theme | what to explore | today's action".
Purpose adapts every step to Umair's real life; the outline is a guide, not a script.

## Break the avoidance loop
For when you know what to do and keep not doing it. 7 days.
Day 1 | Map the loop | Walk through his last avoidance in detail: trigger, feeling, escape, relief, cost. | Notice one avoidance moment today and write down what he felt right before it.
Day 2 | The feeling underneath | Avoidance is escape from a feeling (fear of failing, boredom, overwhelm, self-doubt). Find which one is his. | When the urge to escape comes, name the feeling out loud before doing anything.
Day 3 | Shrink the start | Starting is the hardest part. Make the first step almost too small to fail. | One if-then plan with a tiny start (one page, five minutes) at a set time.
Day 4 | Friction | Make the escape harder and the task easier: phone, place, setup. | Change one thing in his environment before his study time.
Day 5 | After a slip | Self-criticism after a slip feeds the loop; self-compassion breaks it. | After any slip today, talk to himself the way he'd talk to a friend, then restart with the tiny step.
Day 6 | Who you're becoming | Every kept small promise is a vote for the person he wants to be. | Keep the tiny promise again and notice what it says about him.
Day 7 | Review | What worked for him, what didn't, what the loop looks like now. | Choose the one practice he'll keep for the next two weeks.

## Worth beyond output
For when your self-esteem rises and falls with what you get done. 7 days.
Day 1 | Two scales | Untangle what he does from what he's worth. Where did he learn they were the same? | Notice each time today he judges his worth by his output.
Day 2 | The inner critic | Give the critical voice a name and notice its favorite lines. Whose voice does it sound like? | When the critic speaks, answer it once with a fairer sentence.
Day 3 | The friend test | How would he treat a friend in his exact situation? | Write himself three sentences the way a good friend would.
Day 4 | Evidence | Collect real evidence of his strengths and character, from his own history. | Write down three things he's done that took courage or care.
Day 5 | Living a value | Self-respect grows from acting on values, not from results. | One small action today that expresses his top value.
Day 6 | Building khudi | Iqbal's idea of a strong, self-respecting self that doesn't wait for permission. What would that self do this week? | One thing he's been waiting for permission to do; do a small version.
Day 7 | Review | How he talks to himself now vs a week ago. | Choose the practice he'll keep.

## Calm under exam pressure
For anxiety before exams and results. 7 days.
Day 1 | What's in your control | The Stoic split: effort, plan and response vs results and other people. | List what's in his control for the coming exam, and drop one thing that isn't.
Day 2 | Effort, not fruits | The Gita's idea: full effort, released attachment to the result. | Before studying, set the intention for the effort, not the outcome.
Day 3 | Worry time | Give worry a set 15-minute slot instead of all day. | Schedule one worry slot; postpone worries to it.
Day 4 | The body | Sleep, food, movement and slow breathing change how anxious he feels. | One body anchor: a fixed sleep time or a short walk before studying.
Day 5 | A plan that calms | A concrete plan reduces anxiety more than more worrying. | Turn the next three study days into if-then plans.
Day 6 | The worst case | Walk through the feared outcome calmly: what would actually happen, and how would he cope? | Write his "even if" sentence: "Even if I fail, I will…"
Day 7 | Review | What calmed him, what didn't. | Choose his pre-exam routine.

## Discipline foundation
For building steady, reliable self-discipline. 14 days.
Day 1 | Why discipline | What discipline would give him, tied to his future self. | Pick one keystone habit to build first.
Day 2 | One anchor | A fixed daily anchor (wake time or sleep time) steadies everything else. | Set the anchor and keep it tonight or tomorrow.
Day 3 | Tiny start | Start the keystone habit absurdly small. | Do the tiny version at a set time.
Day 4 | Environment | Design the space so the good choice is the easy one. | Change one thing in his room or phone.
Day 5 | Track honestly | Keeping score honestly (not perfectly) builds self-trust. | Mark each day kept or not, no judgment.
Day 6 | The first slip | Slips are normal; the rule is to get back the very next day. | Plan exactly what he'll do the day after a miss.
Day 7 | Week review | What helped, what got in the way. | Adjust the habit's time or size.
Day 8 | Energy | When is he sharpest? Match hard tasks to high-energy hours. | Do his hardest task in his best hour.
Day 9 | Temptation | Name his main escape and the trigger before it. | One if-then plan for the trigger.
Day 10 | Grow the bar | Raise the habit a little now that it's steady. | The slightly bigger version.
Day 11 | Rest | Discipline includes real rest; burnout isn't discipline. | One guilt-free rest block.
Day 12 | Identity | "I'm becoming someone who…" What does the evidence say now? | Write his identity sentence.
Day 13 | Second habit | Stack a second small habit onto the first. | Do both together.
Day 14 | Review | What two weeks taught him about how he works. | Choose what to keep and what's next.

## Understanding emotions
For building EQ: knowing and managing your emotions and reading others. 7 days.
Day 1 | Naming precisely | Going beyond "good" and "bad" to precise feelings. | Three times today, name the exact feeling.
Day 2 | The body knows first | Where emotions show up physically for him. | Notice one emotion in the body before it takes over.
Day 3 | Triggers | What reliably sets off his anger, shame, or anxiety. | Note one trigger and his reaction.
Day 4 | The pause | Space between feeling and reacting. | Pause and take three slow breaths before responding in one tense moment.
Day 5 | Reading others | What might the other person be feeling and needing? | In one interaction, guess the other person's feeling, and check if appropriate.
Day 6 | Saying it well | Observation, feeling, need, request (Nonviolent Communication). | Express one real feeling to someone using this shape.
Day 7 | Review | What changed in how he notices and handles emotions. | Choose the practice he'll keep.

## Hard conversations at home
For a conversation you've been avoiding with family. 7 days.
Day 1 | What you really want | What he wants from the conversation, and what he's afraid of. | Write the one sentence he most needs them to hear.
Day 2 | Their side | Their fears, pressures and love, from their point of view. | Write what they might say, as fairly as possible.
Day 3 | Needs under positions | What each side needs underneath the argument. | Name his need and their need in one line each.
Day 4 | Practice | Role-play the conversation in practice mode. | One practice round with Purpose.
Day 5 | Timing and setting | When and where it has the best chance. | Choose the day, time and place.
Day 6 | The conversation | Final preparation and courage. | Have the conversation, or the first part of it.
Day 7 | Debrief | What happened, what he learned, and what repair or next step is needed. | One follow-up action.

## Finding direction
For when you feel lost or unsure what your life is for. 7 days.
Day 1 | Your values | What matters most, from his own picks and stories. | Notice when he felt most alive this week and why.
Day 2 | Energy audit | What gives energy, what drains it. | Log three energy givers and three drains today.
Day 3 | Sources of meaning | Frankl's three paths: meaningful work, love and connection, and the attitude we take to suffering. | Do one small thing on the path he's neglecting.
Day 4 | Future self letter | Writing from five years ahead. | Write a short letter from his future self.
Day 5 | Small experiments | Direction is found by trying, not just thinking. | Pick one small experiment toward something that pulls him.
Day 6 | Obstacles | What's really in the way: fear, family expectations, money, time. | Name the biggest obstacle and one first move around it.
Day 7 | Review | What became clearer. | Choose his next experiment.

## Keep your word to yourself
For when the promises you make to yourself keep breaking. 7 days.
Day 1 | Your record | Look at his real promise history: which kinds he keeps, which he breaks, and when. Patterns, not verdicts. | Pick the kind of promise he keeps most reliably and say why.
Day 2 | Too big for a bad day | A promise sized for his best day breaks on an ordinary one. | Rewrite one broken promise at the size he would still keep on a bad day.
Day 3 | The obstacle plan | Name the likeliest thing to get in the way and decide in advance what he will do (an if-then). | Make one promise with an if-then for its likeliest obstacle.
Day 4 | Friction | Remove one step between him and the promise, or add one between him and the escape. | Change one thing in his space or on his phone before the promise is due.
Day 5 | When it breaks | A broken promise is data. What exactly happened? Renegotiate instead of dropping it silently. | If a promise broke, say what happened and renegotiate it; if none broke, name what protected it.
Day 6 | Small deposits | Self-trust grows from promises kept, not from big resolutions. | Keep one very small promise and notice what it feels like to say "done".
Day 7 | Review | Which sizes and times he keeps and which he does not. | Choose his promise rule for the next two weeks.

## Catch your own excuses
For when you notice yourself explaining why not. 7 days.
Day 1 | Collect the lines | The sentences he tells himself when he puts something off ("from Monday", "I work better under pressure"). | Write down every excuse line he hears himself say today, in his exact words.
Day 2 | Real reason or story | Some reasons are real constraints and some are stories. A test: would it still hold if the task took ten minutes? | Test one line with that question.
Day 3 | What the line protects | What does the excuse spare him from feeling? | Name the feeling under one excuse.
Day 4 | A fairer sentence | Not an argument with the voice, just a fairer sentence to say back. | Say the fairer sentence once, when the line shows up.
Day 5 | Test it | Turn the excuse into an experiment: if it is true, what happens when he tries for ten minutes? | Run one ten-minute test and write down what really happened.
Day 6 | Rest or avoidance | Rest restores; avoidance postpones. How to tell them apart in his own day. | Take one deliberate rest and one deliberate start, and compare how each feels.
Day 7 | Review | Which lines are his regulars, and what works against them. | Choose the one practice he will keep.

## Live your values this week
For when what you value and how your weeks go don't match. 7 days.
Day 1 | Your top values | His values in his own order, and what each looks like in an ordinary day. | Write one sentence per top value: what it would look like this week.
Day 2 | Last week, honestly | Where did his time and attention actually go? Which values were served and which went hungry? | Sketch last week in five lines and mark which value each line served.
Day 3 | Trade-off or drift | Competing values are normal; unnoticed drift is not. Was the neglect chosen or accidental? | Name one value that went hungry and decide whether that was a choice.
Day 4 | One small act | The smallest act that serves the neglected value. | Do it today and note what it cost him.
Day 5 | Protect it | Put that act into the week with a real time and place. | Schedule it with an if-then for the likeliest obstacle.
Day 6 | Do it | Live the plan, and adjust if the week pushes back. | Do the scheduled act.
Day 7 | Review | What changed when a value got time. | Decide how next week should be shaped by his values.
